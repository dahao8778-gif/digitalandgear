Dust allergy is really dust mite allergy: it's the mites' debris and fine skin-flake particles — mostly 10 to 40 microns — that trigger the sneezing, itchy eyes and congested mornings. Those particles are 30-100x larger than what True HEPA media is certified to trap, so the technology question is settled. The real question is which unit, sized to which room, and that's where most buyers get it wrong.

The Habits That Double the Effect

A purifier cleans the air, not your surfaces. Combine it with these and mite exposure drops dramatically:

  • ✓ Zippered mite-proof covers on mattress and pillows — the single biggest win, since beds are mite headquarters.
  • ✓ Wash bedding weekly at 130°F+ to kill mites and rinse out their debris.
  • ✓ Keep indoor humidity under 50% — mites can't thrive in dry air (if you also run a humidifier, see purifier vs humidifier).
  • ✓ Vacuum with a sealed HEPA vacuum so you don't re-aerosolize what you picked up.
  • ✓ Run the purifier continuously on auto or medium; dust re-enters every time the door opens.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do air purifiers help with dust allergies?

Yes — mite debris (10-40 microns) is far larger than HEPA's 0.3-micron test particle, so a properly sized unit steadily removes it from the room's air.

What CADR do I need?

Dust CADR ≥ two-thirds of room square footage. 150 sq ft → 100+; 450 sq ft → 300+.

Will it remove dust from shelves?

No — purifiers catch particles before they settle. Surfaces still need wiping; the unit keeps new dust from accumulating as fast.

Should it run all night?

Yes, especially in the bedroom on sleep mode — you spend 8 hours breathing next to bedding.

Breathe Easier This Week

Size the unit to the room, run it continuously, and add mite-proof covers — most allergy sufferers feel the difference within one to two weeks.
